Executive decision summary

Approve a controlled experiment with a stop-loss, not a speculative scale-up.

The ask is a gated Coverley pilot of Store All Valet. One SKU, BBD $30 per occupied warehouse bin, inbound trips free, outbound $50 per order. Who it is for

Household overflow is the engine. Ross students are a real Coverley segment — modelled as stress, not the headline LTV.

Primary customer Locked

A Coverley household (owner or renter) with about three to eight bins of household overflow who would not rent a full Store All unit, and who will pay a premium for collection, secure storage and scheduled return. Base-case tenure is household self-storage tenure: 3% monthly account churn (~33 months).

Coverley houses a material number of Ross University students for the academic year. They sit inside the 1,200-home estate, not in a separate adjacent geofence. Recurring warehouse storage is the engine. Inbound is free. Outbound is a priced trip.

Concept in one line: Store All Valet is doorstep by-the-bin storage for Coverley households who need a few totes, not a unit. We deliver empty bins, collect them when packed, store them on racking in Wildey, and return selected bins on request.

UK/EU box-valet pattern: free inbound, paid outbound (Express Storage return £15; BOXIE24 returns from ~€20). $50 is the trip deterrent. Hold fees and no-shows are not in the base P&L.

Revenue model

  • Primary (~96% of steady-state gross): $30 per bin actually in the warehouse.
  • Secondary (~4%): $50 outbound orders at 1.5 per household per year.
  • Not charged: empty drop, first collection, later re-store / add-more.
  • Not the model: per-bin retrieval fees, furniture, or a unit disguised as valet.

At 85% occupancy the P&L is about BBD $12,750 storage + $483 retrieval = $13,233 gross per month. Storage pays rent, crew and van. Outbound only partly covers a loaded trip — that is accepted, and is a watch metric, not a stop.

Who pays, and for what

  • Customer account: phone + booking ref; billed on Store All Plug’n Pay (no in-app Stripe in the pilot).
  • Bin in Wildey: billing unit for storage.
  • Outbound order: billing unit for returns and close-outs.
  • Household base: 5.5 bins ($165/mo). Ross stress: 8 bins ($240/mo).
  • Confirmed (Gate 0): prepaid first month on reserved bin count + card on file + empty-drop date within 45 days. Prepaid credits to the first warehouse month.

Sales and distribution

Where demand comes from

  • Geofence: The Villages at Coverley only for the pilot (1,200 homes, including Ross students living on the estate).
  • Direct digital: public pricing + reservation; pay-to-confirm via Store All Plug’n Pay (no field sales team).
  • Estate / community: property-management and resident channels.
  • Store All adjacency: 1–6 bin overflow that does not justify a unit; show both at 7–8; unit at 9+ or furniture.

How a sale closes

  • Campaign / referral → pricing page (one SKU, $30 / $50, prepaid first month)
  • Customer selects bin count + window → accepts T&Cs
  • Confirmed only with prepaid first month + card + date within 45 days
  • Empty bins delivered → 7 days to book collection → we collect → rack in Wildey → $30 starts
  • Later: $50 outbound via phone / booking ref; 7-day borrow then re-store free or close

Business-model test for the pilot

The pilot succeeds as a business only if (1) prepaid demand hits Gate 0 before the $1,500 bay is taken, (2) household storage ARPU and tenure support ~$50 contribution per account, and (3) outbound volume does not consume the single FTE (watch hours, do not pretend loaded trip cost will be ≤ $50). Change fees only with measured conversion and loaded hours.

Technology, billing and who runs it

The offer is locked. The till is Plug’n Pay. The operating system is the Store All Valet booking and staff system already built for this pilot.

Software was a $6,000 setup line and $200/month without saying what that buys. The commercial model only works if occupancy in Wildey is the billing meter, and if unpaid web bookings cannot count as Gate 0 demand.

What already exists Built

  • Public booking site: Coverley geofence, one SKU, locked $30 / $50 copy, lookup by phone or booking ref. Customers never sign in.
  • Bin as unit of truth: barcode scans at the door and the rack; legal transitions only; chain of custody on every move.
  • Three staff surfaces: admin (queue, assign bins, reports), warehouse (put-away / scan-out), driver (jobs board). Phone camera is the scanner.
  • Hosted stack: Vercel + Supabase Postgres. Occupancy and job activity already report in admin.
  • Customer comms: email (and optional SMS) on booking and job completion, once keys are set. 7-day pack copy is in those messages.

What this app does not do

  • No till: it does not take the prepaid first month, store a card, or invoice $30 / $50 / hold / no-show. That is Store All Plug’n Pay, keyed by booking ref.
  • No clock enforcement: the 7-day pack and borrow windows are copy and ops discipline, not a blocking timer.
  • No seals or weight capture: 25 kg and tamper seals stay a driver checklist until counsel and ops lock a form.
  • Not in this pilot: SiteLink, in-app Stripe, customer login, route optimisation, multi-site.

The $6,000 ops-setup line is barcodes, phones, Plug’n Pay wiring and site copy. It is not a new warehouse system. The Valet build is already sunk and sits outside the $72k ceiling.

Recommended month-six decision rule (payback-aware)

Scale consideration Occupancy above break-even; household churn and must-hit service quality green; actual contribution within 15% of this model; no unresolved legal/claims/safety issue; and updated forecast shows cumulative cash recovery within 24 months of launch. FTE #2 is costed in the next paper, not this one.

Extend / reprice Demand above break-even but watch metrics (hours, borrow share, CAC) amber, with a corrective plan (including $50 outbound change if hours stay red-ish).

Stop Occupancy below ~54% without credible pipeline; cash forecast exceeds BBD $72,000; claims/safety unacceptable; or customer proposition cannot cover actual operating cost.

Green occupancy at month 6 is not enough if payback has slipped beyond two years. Loaded trip cost above $50 is not, by itself, a stop.
